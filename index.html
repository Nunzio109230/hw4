THE FRAMEWORK

Two models, two behaviors.

We compare a positive-score model with a symmetric, zero-mean random walk.

B Model 01

Bernoulli model

A step either contributes one point or zero.

\(X_i\in\{0,1\}\)
\(P(X_i=1)\)\(p\)
\(P(X_i=0)\)\(1-p\)
Mean\(\mu=p\)
Variance\(\sigma^2=p(1-p)\)

The total score counts successful steps. Its expected value increases linearly with \(n\).

R Model 02

Rademacher model

Every step adds or subtracts one point with equal probability.

\(X_i\in\{-1,+1\}\)
\(P(X_i=+1)\)\(\frac12\)
\(P(X_i=-1)\)\(\frac12\)
Mean\(\mu=0\)
Variance\(\sigma^2=1\)

Positive and negative increments balance on average, but the cumulative score still fluctuates.

The models are mathematical descriptions of random scores. Their real-world meaning depends on how a score is defined in the application.

MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND

From random steps to limit theorems.

Independent and identically distributed increments connect finite simulations to general probabilistic results.

01

Cumulative score

\(S_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\)

Since the increments are independent and identically distributed, with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), linearity of expectation and independence imply:

Expected value\(E[S_n]=n\mu\)
Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(S_n)=n\sigma^2\)
02

Law of Large Numbers

\( \frac{S_n}{n}\xrightarrow{P}\mu \)

The sample average converges in probability to the expected increment. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the probability that it differs from \(\mu\) by more than \(\varepsilon\) tends to zero.

Mean\(E[S_n/n]=\mu\)
Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(S_n/n)=\sigma^2/n\)

Chebyshev's inequality gives \(P(|S_n/n-\mu|\geq\varepsilon)\leq \sigma^2/(n\varepsilon^2)\).

03

Square-root scaling

\( \operatorname{Var}(S_n/\sqrt n)=\sigma^2 \)

Dividing by \(\sqrt n\) preserves the scale of random fluctuations. Unlike \(S_n/n\), this quantity does not generally concentrate at a constant.

Mean\(E[S_n/\sqrt n]=\sqrt n\,\mu\)
Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(S_n/\sqrt n)=\sigma^2\)

For Bernoulli increments with \(p>0\), \(S_n/\sqrt n\) has a growing mean. For a zero-mean Rademacher process, it has mean zero and variance one.

04

Central Limit Theorem

\( \frac{S_n-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1) \)

For independent, identically distributed increments with finite positive variance, the standardized sum converges in distribution to a standard normal random variable.

Special case: Rademacher
Here \(\mu=0\) and \(\sigma^2=1\). Therefore, \(S_n/\sqrt n\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)\).
APPLICATION

Interpreting systems and attackers.

The mathematical results describe aggregate scores; a practical interpretation requires a clear model of how attackers affect the increments.

n

System size / number of steps

In this model, \(n\) is the number of increments. Larger \(n\) increases the scale of the raw cumulative score, while the average \(S_n/n\) becomes more concentrated around \(\mu\).

m

Number of attackers

The parameter \(m\) describes the number of attackers in the scenario. The assignment does not specify a mathematical relationship between \(m\) and the distribution of \(X_i\), so the simulation keeps \(m\) contextual rather than changing the probabilities automatically.

Δ

Positive vs. symmetric increments

Bernoulli increments have a positive mean when \(p>0\). Rademacher increments have zero mean, so gains and losses balance on average, even though individual trajectories can move far from zero.

i
Modelling assumption

To study the effect of \(m\) quantitatively, an additional assumption is needed, such as a specified function \(p=p(m)\), or a rule describing how attackers contribute to each increment. This relationship should be justified rather than assumed without explanation.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What should the experiments reveal?

01

The mean and variance of the raw score grow as \(n\mu\) and \(n\sigma^2\).

02

The average score \(S_n/n\) concentrates around \(\mu\), illustrating the LLN.

03

04

For Rademacher increments, \(S_n/\sqrt n\) approaches a standard normal distribution.
